Taiwanese mahjong rules

Taiwanese mahjong is played with sixteen-tile hands and scored in tai. A complete hand of five sets and a pair can win at any tai count — there is no minimum, and no ready hand needs to be announced before winning.

The tiles

  • The set has 144 tiles: four copies of each of the 34 ordinary kinds — 27 numbered kinds (1 to 9 in characters, circles, and bamboo) and seven honors (the four winds and three dragons) — plus eight flower tiles.
  • The eight flower tiles are the four plants (plum, orchid, chrysanthemum, bamboo plant) and the four seasons (spring, summer, autumn, winter). Together they are called flowers.

The sixteen-tile hand

  • Each player is dealt sixteen tiles, and the dealer one more. A winning hand is five sets and a pair, completed by the seventeenth tile.
  • A set is a sequence of three numbers in one suit, a triplet of one kind, or a quad; a declared quad draws a replacement tile.
  • A discard may be claimed for a sequence only by the player whose turn comes next, and for a triplet or a quad from any seat. A claimed tile cannot be swapped back in the discard right after the claim.

Tai and scoring

  • A hand's score is counted in tai. Each award a hand earns — each flower, each wind or dragon triplet, the way the hand was kept and won — adds tai, and the tai are added together; where a bigger award contains a smaller one, the bigger replaces the smaller instead of stacking.
  • There is no minimum tai: a complete hand with no tai at all can still win, and still collects the base payment.

Flowers

  • A flower never joins a set or the pair. When one comes to you, you set it aside face-up beside your hand and draw another tile in its place, so your hand keeps its proper size.
  • Every flower earns one tai when you win, whichever plant or season it is.

Payments

  • Each payment is the base of 30 points plus 10 points for each of the hand's tai.
  • A win on a discard is paid by the discarder alone. A self-draw win is paid by all three opponents.
  • Any payment that involves the dealer — the dealer paying, or the dealer winning — adds a dealer bonus to that payment only: one extra tai, plus two more for each time the dealer has repeated.

The match

  • A full game runs through four rounds — East, South, West and North — and in each round every player deals once in turn.
  • The dealer repeats after winning a hand and after a drawn hand, and each repeat raises the repeat count used in that dealer's payments. A win by a player who is not the dealer passes the deal and resets the repeat count to zero; at this table the deal passes after ten repeats even if the dealer wins again.
  • Each player starts the game with 1,000 points. Payments move points from the players who pay to the player who won, so after every hand the four scores still add up to 4,000.
