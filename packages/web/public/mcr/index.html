Mahjong Competition Rules (MCR)

MCR is scored in fan, counted from the named patterns a finished hand contains. A hand needs at least eight fan from its patterns to win; flower and season points are added after that minimum is met.

The tiles

  • The set has 144 tiles: four copies of each of the 34 ordinary kinds — 27 numbered kinds (1 to 9 in characters, circles, and bamboo) and seven honors (the four winds and three dragons) — plus eight flower and season tiles.
  • The 1 and the 9 of a numbered suit are its terminals; the numbers from 2 to 8 are simples. An honor has no number and can only match copies of itself.

The winning shape

  • A normal winning hand has fourteen tiles: four sets and a pair. A set is a sequence of three numbers in one suit, a triplet of one kind, or a quad; a declared quad draws a replacement tile.
  • Three special structures win without four sets and a pair: seven pairs, thirteen orphans, and the knitted hands.

Patterns and fan

  • A pattern is a named feature of a finished hand. The scoring list has 81 patterns, grouped in families: honors, triplets and quads, sequences, tile composition, special structures, concealment and winning, and small combinations.
  • Every pattern is worth a set number of fan, and a hand's fan is the sum of its patterns. When patterns overlap, each pattern is counted once: a higher pattern replaces the lower patterns it is made from.
  • A finished hand can only be declared a win if its patterns add up to at least eight fan. A finished hand that contains no pattern at all is awarded Chicken Hand, worth exactly eight fan, so it can win after all.

Flowers and seasons

  • When you draw a flower or a season, you show it beside your tiles and immediately draw a replacement, so it never takes a place among the tiles you are building with.
  • Each flower or season you show adds one point to a winning hand, but only after the hand has reached the eight-fan minimum from its patterns; flower points never help a hand reach the minimum.

Payments

  • Every player who did not win pays 8 base points toward the win.
  • On a win from another player's thrown tile, that player — the discarder — also pays the full hand value, the hand's fan plus its flower points; the other two players pay only the base. On a self-draw win, every opponent pays the base plus the hand value.
  • Being the dealer makes no difference to any of these amounts; the deal carries no multiplier.

The match

  • A full match has four rounds — East, South, West and North — and each round has four hands, one with each player as dealer. Shorter matches that stop after the East round, or after East and South, use exactly the same rules for every hand.
  • The deal passes to the next player after every hand; the dealer never keeps the deal for a repeat hand.
  • Every player starts a match with a score of zero. Payments move points from the players who lost a hand to the player who won it, so the four scores always add up to zero.
